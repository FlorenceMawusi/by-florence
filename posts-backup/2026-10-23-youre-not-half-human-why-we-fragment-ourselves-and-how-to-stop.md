# You're Not Half Human: Why We Fragment Ourselves (and How to Stop)
_Reclaiming your emotional, spiritual, and physical self as one whole person_

## The Surprise Factor

Growing up in a sheltered environment, I saw the world mostly as black or white. But after leaving home, I began to experience different aspects of life no one told me I’ll encounter. Sometimes I lose something precious to me. I have lost lots of things physically, mentally, emotionally, and relationally. Sometimes life has taken an unexpected turn, very different than the script I was told will happen. Nevertheless, I am grateful for all these experiences because firstly it’s made me who I am and secondly I have gained just as much as I’ve lost and I’m grateful for that. 

## Self Judgement vs Other

During this process of gaining some and losing some (which I’m sure happens to every single human), I realize that we usually find ourselves in circumstances outside control. When this happens, we tend to extend grace to ourselves, but we don’t extend the same grace to others.

For example, Bella might get confused and worried when she gains weight out of nowhere. However, the moment she steps out and see an overweight stranger, the first thought that might come to mind is “how could he allow himself to get to this?”

This particular thought makes it sound like the stranger “allowed” it. But in reality, the stranger probably was just living a normal life. The weight just creeped in. And now his body is heavier. In fact what I have realized is, to get the result or goal you want in our society, you need to go against the grain of what our environment set us up to do.

## The world design 

Say you were born into a family with a history of diabetes: you’ve already inherited the high blood glucose gene. Now you grow up in a community (family/neighbors/school/grocery) that doesn’t promote whole food eating and exercising. It’s even worse for Gen Alpha and later Gen Zs. They’re distracted with iPads the moment their parents needed a break. At least my generation had the gift of playing outdoor games like ‘ampe’ (a Ghanaian game played by young girls), football, and skipping, tug of war, ‘mummy and daddy’ (we pretended to be adult moms and dads to kids). Oh! What fun we had!

Anyways, so we grow up with ultra processed foods and a sedentary lifestyle at a young age. These habits are replicated, encouraged, and normalized. In fact sometimes people are seen as the weird ones for engaging in healthy trends. You know that person we say eats grass for breakfast lunch and dinner?! Why would they want to do that?! Right?! Or the person who goes for 3 to 5 hour long walks a day. Don’t they have a job in our existing economy? Such health stuff is only reserved for the super rich who don’t have to think about bills!

## Health for the Super Rich

Oh, and don’t even get me started on the organic vs ‘normal’ groceries. You need to pay a premium so your food doesn’t get sprayed with pesticides that could harm your health. Meanwhile, organic groceries are supposed to be the norm. Why give me what’s normal and put a premium on it?!

And guess what? Naturopathic and lifestyle healthcare are not covered by insurance. You’ll have spend at least $2000 to see a naturopathic doctor. Unbelievable!

Oh and don’t even get me started on all these fancy health bars, supplements that are more expensive than a restaurant meal

Yes, all these are crazy! Good health has become a luxury in our society. So is it really surprising that there is an obesity epidemic? The world we live in is designed exactly for this. Why did we design the world this way? Ladies and gentlemen, that’s a topic for another day. 

So you see, people truly find themselves in circumstances they didn’t sign up for, locked in by habits and emotional memory of what feels right. Changing is hard, not impossible, but hard. So kudos to all those putting their health first even when their environment doesn’t support it. I just hope that when we see others struggling we will give them grace. Because it could have been you if you had their exact life. 

All human circumstances happen to any of us. We may not experience some of them, but it is a possibility that we might. Yes it is. And it helps when we don’t harbor judgment. Because it makes it easier to accept ourselves when we face the same predicament. I’ll make another post on how to let judgement go, but this is a good place to start. 

## The human experience 

Being stuck in a certain area of life doesn’t make you less human. In fact it makes you fully human to experience the diversity of life; the good, the bad, and the ugly. 

I get it, we all don’t want to experience pain or feel like we’re regressing. Some religious beliefs and self help movements haven’t helped either. They promote the idea of needing to climb the ladder of success ASAP. And if you have a setback then that’s bad. Real bad. And it’s also your fault, you pinhead. You need to be ahead of your peers. Oh you definitely don’t want to be behind, while they’re making it in the world, getting that new job, buying their new house, owning their first private jet yk.

That’s a lot of pressure isn’t it? That’s why I cherish authors such as Mark Manson and Brene Brown. They bring fresh perspectives to the self help industry. They promote values like gratitude, courage, vulnerability, and kindness, which, thankfully, don’t have the same ring as “winning at all costs”.

## Life as a Playground

Maybe a different way of looking at life might help with that fear. That is, seeing life as a playground, a place where you get to experiment, experience what’s possible, explore anything and everything, get curious, break things, touch the fire, let it burn, let it heal too. I like this way of looking at life. It makes me daring and not afraid to fail. I mean I still have my bad days but I pick myself up more quickly than I used to.

Remember how we were as kids? Definitely more adventurous, always getting our parents in trouble haha! We should bring back this spirit. Remember that we’re here to live the full human experience. Failing at something doesn’t make us half human after all.
