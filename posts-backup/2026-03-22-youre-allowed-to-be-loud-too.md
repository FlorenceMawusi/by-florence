# You're Allowed to Be Loud Too 
_How I stopped matching everyone else's energy and started broadcasting my own_

[![boy singing on microphone with pop filter](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1453738773917-9c3eff1db985?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3wzMDAzMzh8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHwzfHxsb3VkfGVufDB8fHx8MTc3Mzg2ODk3NHww&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080)](<https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1453738773917-9c3eff1db985?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3wzMDAzMzh8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHwzfHxsb3VkfGVufDB8fHx8MTc3Mzg2ODk3NHww&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080>)Photo by [Jason Rosewell](<https://unsplash.com/@jasonrosewell>) on [Unsplash](<https://unsplash.com>)

Ever been in a room where one person’s energy just… takes over?

Like, their mood becomes the weather. They’re happy? Great, everyone breathes. 

They’re upset? Suddenly you’re scanning the room, adjusting your posture, choosing your words carefully. 

Their frequency is the only one playing, and you? You’re just tuning in.

If you grew up like that — with a guardian whose energy was the loudest in the house — you know exactly what I’m talking about. 

You learned to read the room before you learned to read yourself. You became an expert at matching someone else’s frequency. Their comfort was the priority. Your signal? Barely a whisper.

That skill follows you everywhere.

## The Invisible Pattern

You leave that house. You grow up. You get into friendships, jobs, relationships. And without even realizing it, you’re still doing the same thing — scanning, adjusting, shrinking. Someone else’s energy fills the space, and yours just… goes quiet.

Somewhere deep in your wiring, you learned that there’s only room for one frequency at a time. And it’s not yours.

You might not even notice it at first. It shows up in small ways. You clean up messes that aren’t yours. You swallow your discomfort because saying something feels like too much. You become the person who holds everything together while slowly falling apart inside.

Sound familiar?

## Two Frequencies, One Space

Here’s what I’ve been learning lately: a healthy relationship isn’t one frequency dominating and the other adjusting. It’s two frequencies existing in the same space. Both broadcasting. Both being heard.

That sounds simple. It’s not.

Because when you’ve spent your whole life tuning into other people, learning to broadcast your own signal feels almost… rude? Like, wait — I’m allowed to say I’m uncomfortable? I’m allowed to not clean that up? I’m allowed to have needs that take up space?

Yes. You are.

And the wild part is — when you start doing this, the right person doesn’t experience it as a threat. They experience it as clarity. They’re not losing something when you speak up. They’re gaining a real partner instead of a performer.

## The Puzzle Piece Problem

But here’s where it gets real. Two frequencies in one space means they’re going to bump into each other. Your worldview and theirs won’t always match. Your instincts might pull in different directions. And when that happens, the fairy tale falls apart a little.

Because we’ve been sold this idea that if it’s right, the puzzle pieces just click. No friction. No adjustment. Everything fits perfectly from day one.

That’s a lie.

Real puzzle pieces don’t come pre-cut for each other. They have rough edges. Weird shapes. Parts that don’t seem like they belong together at first glance. And the work — the real work — is figuring out how to fit them together without either piece losing its shape.

That’s where workable compromise comes in.

## Workable Compromise (Not Sacrifice)

There’s a big difference between compromise and sacrifice. Sacrifice is one person reshaping their entire piece to fit the other’s. It looks like peace, but it’s actually just silence. One frequency went quiet again.

Workable compromise is different. Both pieces adjust a little. Both people keep their core shape. Nobody disappears. Nobody dominates. You find the edges where you can meet, and you build from there.

It sounds like: “I see things this way, you see them that way. How do we make something that works for both of us?”

It doesn’t sound like: “Fine, I’ll just do it your way.”

And honestly? Workable compromise is harder. It takes more conversations. More patience. More willingness to sit in the discomfort of not having it figured out yet. But what it builds is so much stronger than the alternative — because both people are actually in it. Not just one person carrying the whole thing while the other rides along.

## Breaking the Generational Loop

If you grew up in a house where one frequency ruled, you know what’s at stake. Because without doing this work, the pattern just repeats. You become the loud frequency, or you find someone who is. Either way, someone’s signal gets drowned out.

But when you learn to hold your own frequency steady — when you learn to speak up, set boundaries, and take up space without guilt — you’re not just changing your relationship. You’re changing the template.

The next generation won’t have to grow up scanning the room to survive. They’ll grow up knowing their frequency matters too. That their feelings are data, not inconvenience. That relationships are spaces where everyone gets to be heard.

And that? That’s worth every uncomfortable conversation. Every awkward boundary. Every moment where you have to remind yourself that your signal deserves airtime too.

## The Real Flex

The real flex isn’t finding someone whose puzzle piece clicks perfectly on day one. The real flex is finding someone willing to do the work of fitting the pieces together — and being someone who’s willing to do that work too.

Two whole people. Two full frequencies. One shared space.

That’s not a fairy tale. That’s better. That’s real.
