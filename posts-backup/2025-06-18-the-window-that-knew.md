# The Window That Knew
_A Window, A Heart, A Farewell_

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!JEfq!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F3827ac93-daa5-46d3-9fd8-b8b9eff7cb54_1024x536.png)](<https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!JEfq!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F3827ac93-daa5-46d3-9fd8-b8b9eff7cb54_1024x536.png>)

Passing by  
not the same way.

The window stared at me,  
with no glass,  
with memory.

Its panes once shimmered  
with soft golden haze  
when midnight curled around the building  
like a cat settling in.

Back then,  
its eyes glowed  
whispers of warmth  
captive behind old wood and fogged breath.

Only when the dimness came  
did I see it  
bare, brittle,  
no longer holding the light  
you took with you.

It was hollow.  
And different.  
And cold.

When I saw  
that the window was hollow,  
my heart unclenched,  
finally brave enough  
to show me the truth:

My world had shifted.  
A seam had split.  
A piece of me   
quietly taken  
before I even noticed.

Way before it was obvious.

Before the final goodbyes,  
when I looked at your face  
and believed  
that my heart was still whole.

But later,  
passing that same window  
I didn’t need to look within.

My heart rose up,  
unannounced,  
spilling truth and ache  
all at once.

And there it was  
the missing part,  
etched like frost  
on the glass I once ignored.

The weight of presence  
I hadn’t known I leaned into.

Grief comes like fog  
no warning,  
no language.

I still don’t grasp it.  
It baffles me.

How I can stand before a human,  
know their name,  
know the shell they walk in,  
yet feel the inexplicable blanket  
of their presence.

How just their nearness  
could awaken  
a sunrise inside me  
a sun with no sky,  
no clock,  
just warmth and color  
bursting behind my ribs.

My chest once floated.  
I was light.  
I was joy.  
I was full.

But I know the cycle.  
Loves will come.  
Loves will go.

And I will ache.  
And release.  
And stitch myself back together  
with laughter and tears and soil.

And maybe  
reunite  
when the stars realign.

But even in the absence,  
I will not forget.

Because my loves  
they gave me beauty  
that wore no mask.

They offered wealth  
that no coin could match.

They returned my youth  
in the way they said my name  
like it was something to keep safe.

They gave me themselves.  
And I,  
in all my trembling,  
gave them me.

And even now,  
as I walk past that window—  
the one that once held your shadow—  
I remember.

They stay in my breath.  
My bones carry their laughter.  
My skin remembers  
the language of their care.

I remember  
their imprint—  
like light  
stained into old wood.

And though I pass by differently now,  
I still pause.  
Still look.  
Still ache.  
And still give thanks.
