# Sovereignty: The Most Life-Changing Skill We Were Never Taught
_What it Really Means to Be Sovereign, and Why “No” Is the Gateway to Freedom_

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!BcZE!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F05c9c899-08c2-4379-9c9b-e2375f1f2fb6_1024x1024.png)](<https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!BcZE!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F05c9c899-08c2-4379-9c9b-e2375f1f2fb6_1024x1024.png>)

Most of us grow up believing that “being a good person” means being agreeable.

Say yes.

Don’t upset anyone.

Don’t inconvenience people.

Don’t have “too many” needs.

Explain yourself.

Justify yourself.

Don’t take up space.

And yet… the moment we start living from this template, something essential dies inside us:

**our sovereignty.**

It wasn’t until I read the first 30 pages of _When I Say No, I Feel Guilty_ (Manuel J. Smith, 1975) that I realized just how deeply I’d been conditioned to believe that saying no was wrong. The book is written simply, almost unassumingly, but the idea inside it shattered a foundational belief in me:

**You have the right to exist without justification.**

It sounds small.

But for me, it split my life into “**before** ” and “**after**.”

* * *

# **What Is Sovereignty?**

Sovereignty is the state of being the **author** , not the **actor** of your life.

It is the recognition that:

  * your desires are legitimate simply because you have them

  * your boundaries don’t require explanation

  * your emotional truth deserves space

  * your needs are not burdens

  * your “no” is enough

  * you do not exist to manage, soothe, or shrink for others

Sovereignty is **self-ownership**.

It is the internal sense of:

> “I am allowed to be here.
> 
> I am allowed to want.
> 
> I am allowed to say no.
> 
> I do not need permission to exist.”

It is the collapse of the belief that you must justify your presence.

* * *

# **Assertiveness Is the Behavioral Expression of Sovereignty**

In _When I Say No, I Feel Guilty_ , Smith introduces a concept that changed my entire identity: **the Assertive Rights** , one of the earliest psychological frameworks that gave people permission to be sovereign.

The core of these rights is breathtakingly simple:

**You have the right to judge your own behavior, thoughts, and emotions.**

**You have the right to offer no reasons or excuses for your behavior.**

**You have the right to say “I don’t know.”**

**You have the right to change your mind.**

**You have the right to be illogical about what you want.**

**You have the right to make mistakes and be imperfect.**

**You have the right to not take responsibility for others’ problems.**

When I first read these and understood what they meant, I felt a shock in my chest, like someone had unearthed an internal law that had been buried under decades of conditioning.

These were **permissions and human rights I never knew I was allowed to have.**

* * *

# **Why Sovereignty Feels So Radical**

Because many of us grew up in environments where:

  * love was conditional

  * approval was currency

  * conflict felt dangerous

  * pleasure required permission

  * personal needs were minimized

  * perfection was demanded

  * authority figures felt towering and absolute

Which means our nervous systems learned:

“Safety comes from pleasing others.”

“Don’t upset anyone.”

“Don’t be too visible.”

“Don’t ask for too much.”

“Don’t contradict authority.”

“Don’t reject requests, it’s rude.”

So when sovereignty comes online, whether gradually or through a quantum change, everything feels new:

Suddenly you can say no.

Suddenly you feel your own preferences.

Suddenly you don’t over-explain.

Suddenly you don’t feel “in trouble.”

Suddenly authority feels human, not giant-sized.

Suddenly you are _in the room_ with people instead of collapsing for them.

It feels like waking up.

* * *

# **Sovereignty Is Also Emotional Intimacy**

True sovereignty is the ability to express:

  * what’s happening inside your body

  * what you want

  * what you don’t want

  * what matters to you

  * what hurts

  * what delights you

  * what you’re imagining

  * what you’re fearing

When you reveal your internal world, without shrinking, defending, or performing, you allow **real connection** to form.

Boundaries are not rejection.

Boundaries are clarity.

Clarity is intimacy.

When I began practicing sovereignty, conversations stopped being about “what should I say?” and started being about:

**“What is true for me right now?”**

This changed everything.

* * *

# **The Three Pillars of Sovereignty**

### **1\. Self-Authority**

You decide what you feel, want, believe, and choose.

No external approval required!!!!!!!!

### **2\. Self-Protection**

You guard your emotional, physical, and psychological space through boundaries, which require no explanation.

“No” is a complete sentence.

### **3\. Self-Expression**

You reveal your inner world honestly and cleanly to be known, even when what you’re feeling doesnt make sense.

* * *

# **Why Saying “No” Is the Ultimate Spiritual Practice**

I didn’t expect this, but sovereignty carries a **spiritual resonance**.

The Bible says, _“Let your yes be yes and your no be no.”_

That’s sovereignty.

Renewing the mind?

Also sovereignty.

Stepping out of fear-based living?

Sovereignty again.

Even the Christian idea of “identity in Christ” is fundamentally about reclaiming internal authority over shame, guilt, and external judgment.

Sovereignty is spiritual because it ends the bondage of **fear**.

It is the moment you stop being shaped by external pressure

and start being shaped by **your internal truth**.

It is what freedom feels like.

* * *

# **Sovereignty Changes Everything**

When you become sovereign:

  * relationships become dialogues, not hierarchies

  * authority figures lose their power to shrink you

  * friendships shift toward reciprocity

  * your voice becomes steady

  * guilt dissolves

  * you self-advocate naturally

  * you become emotionally and somatically awake

  * life starts aligning with who you _really_ are

Sovereignty is adulthood.

The deep internal realization:

> “I belong to myself.”

* * *

# **If You’re Beginning This Journey**

Start small:

  * “No, I don’t want to.”

  * “That doesn’t work for me.”

  * “I need time to think.”

  * “I prefer this instead.”

  * “I’m not available.”

  * “I disagree.”

  * “I’d rather do it this way.”

Every small moment of truth-telling restores your sense of self.

This is how you reclaim your emotional territory.

This is how you build a sovereign life.
