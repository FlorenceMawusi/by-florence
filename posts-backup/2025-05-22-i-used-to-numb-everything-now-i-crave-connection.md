#  I Used to Numb Everything. Now I Crave Connection.
_Reflections from a Recovered Dismissive Avoidant (DA)_

**1\. Opening Thought**  
I used to think I didn’t need people. I numbed everything: emotions, thoughts, even joy. YouTube, food, overworking… anything to avoid feeling. But recovery changed me.

**2\. What I’ve Realized**  
One of the first things I had to face in recovery was this: _I need emotional connection._  
And not just in theory. I feel it in my bones now. I need presence. I need real moments with people. That’s been hard to accept. It challenges everything I thought made me strong.

**3\. The DA Habit of Numbing**  
Back then, if something hurt, I’d scroll. If I felt lonely, I’d binge a series. If someone got too close, I’d pull back. I thought that was control. I thought that was peace. It wasn’t. It was survival. Disconnection was my shield.

**4\. What’s Different Now**  
Now, I sit in the discomfort. I let myself be around people emotionally. I soak in the connection. Sometimes, I even smile at how human it all feels.  
And I’ve noticed something: when I don’t get that emotional nourishment, I’m grumpy. I don’t want to work. My cup’s just empty.

**5\. From Achievement to Affection**  
I used to be driven by achievement, chasing the next job, the next promotion, the next title. That’s how I thought I’d earn love.  
As a child, love often came with conditions: be perfect, cook well, get it right.  
But now, I’m discovering something deeper: my identity is also in the people who love me, and those I love in return.  
Achievement still has a place. It helps fund the life I want, but love doesn’t have to be earned anymore.

**6\. Shame, Flaws, and Being Seen**  
I used to be so ashamed of myself.  
If someone noticed a flaw in me, or even just hinted at my not-enoughness, it triggered a spiral of shame.  
Deep, painful shame that made me want to hide. I'd think, _They finally see who I really am._  
That’s something many of us with dismissive avoidance carry: a hidden fear that if people _really_ knew us, they’d leave.  
Sometimes, I’d imagine people saying mean things about me, not because they were true, but because I feared they could be true, and part of me would believe what they say. _What if it’s true? What if I really am that inadequate?_  
But today, I’m learning that flaws don’t make you unlovable.  
They make you human.  
And being human doesn’t mean being cast out. It means being part of the story.  
Most people aren’t looking for perfect, they’re looking for _real_. For kindness. For decency.  
And if you’re not a toxic brat (which, let's be honest, most of us aren't), people _will_ love you.  
They’ll love the real you.

**7\. A New Kind of Peace**  
The most exciting part about recovery is that I don’t live in emotional chaos anymore.  
Sure, school is hard. Work is hard. Life is still life. But my inner world is calmer. The storms don’t come as often. When they do, I don’t collapse. I don’t panic. I breathe. and I thrive.

**8\. Triggers That Don’t Trigger**  
Fights don’t spiral. Some things that used to wreck me now just… pass.  
I can let go more easily. I feel like I’ve grown a solid ground inside myself.

**9\. Closing Reflection**  
I’m still learning. But if you’re reading this and you feel distant, numb, or guarded, there’s another way to live. And it feels good.  
It feels alive.
