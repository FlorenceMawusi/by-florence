# What Even Is AI?
_On frozen waves, collective consciousness, and the things we still can't explain_

[![black and white robot toy on red wooden table](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1620712943543-bcc4688e7485?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3wzMDAzMzh8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHw1fHxodW1hbiUyMGFpfGVufDB8fHx8MTc3NDA0MTc5M3ww&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080)](<https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1620712943543-bcc4688e7485?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3wzMDAzMzh8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHw1fHxodW1hbiUyMGFpfGVufDB8fHx8MTc3NDA0MTc5M3ww&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080>)Photo by [Andrea De Santis](<https://unsplash.com/@santesson89>) on [Unsplash](<https://unsplash.com>)

Okay so we throw around the term “artificial intelligence” like we all agree on what that means. 

But do we even know what _intelligence_ is in the first place? Like the regular, non-artificial kind? Because if we don’t, then what exactly are we making an artificial version of?

And I don’t think we do. Not fully.

Because when most people say intelligence, they mean the logical stuff. Math. Reasoning. Problem solving. Two plus two is four. That kind of thing. And sure, that’s part of it. But that’s such a small slice of what’s actually going on when a human being navigates the world.

There’s emotional intelligence — and I don’t mean that in the corporate HR way. I mean like, you walk into a room and something feels off before anyone says a word. Or you meet someone and your whole body goes nope. Or you see something beautiful and your chest does that thing. That’s intelligence. That’s your body processing information faster than your conscious mind can keep up with.

There’s gut instinct — that deep knowing in your stomach that has nothing to do with logic. You can’t explain it. You can’t point to the data that led you there. But you _know_. And half the time it’s right.

There’s biological intelligence — your immune system fighting things off without you thinking about it, your body healing a wound, your nervous system deciding fight or flight in milliseconds. That’s intelligence operating at a level we barely think about.

And then there’s that other thing. That thing where you’re just drawn to something for no reason. You love poetry and you don’t know why. You feel called to a certain path and there’s no logical explanation. Something in you just knows what it knows. Where does that come from? Nobody can tell you. But it’s real. And it’s doing something.

So intelligence is logical, emotional, biological, intuitive, embodied, and probably a few other things we haven’t figured out how to name yet. And when we say “artificial intelligence” — we basically took the pattern recognition and logical reasoning slice, scaled it way up, and called it intelligent. Which... okay. But what is it? And what is it not?

## so what is AI actually?

Here’s how I’ve been thinking about it.

The internet is kind of like a collective consciousness or information. It’s alive. Billions of people are reading from it and writing to it at the same time. It evolves in real time. You can add to it. You can pull from it. It’s messy and contradictory and constantly changing. It reflects and reshapes human thought as it happens.

AI — specifically the large language models we’re all using right now — was trained _on_ that collective knowldge or consciousness. But it’s not part of it. Not in the same way. It’s more like a snapshot. A really sophisticated, interactive snapshot of human knowledge and language patterns, frozen at a point in time.

I keep thinking of it as frozen waves. Like someone took a photograph of the ocean. It captures something real — the shape, the movement, the texture. But the ocean has moved on. And the photograph doesn’t move with it.

You can’t write to AI the way you write to the internet. You can talk to it, but nothing changes on its end. The collective doesn’t shift because of your conversation. Whereas if you publish a blog post, tweet something, put out a video — you’re actually contributing to the collective. You’re changing the pool. You’re adding to the living thing.

So AI sits in this weird in-between space. It’s not an individual mind — it has no singular lived experience, no continuous memory, no persistent identity. But it’s also not truly collective because it can’t be updated by everyone simultaneously. 

It’s more like... a distillation. A compressed, static, interactive distillation of what humanity has thought and said and written up to a certain date. Incredibly useful. Genuinely powerful. But fundamentally different in kind from both an individual human mind and the living collective.

## what AI can and can’t create

There are two different kinds of “new.”

There’s combinatorial novelty — taking existing ideas and patterns and recombining them in ways nobody has before. AI can absolutely do this. And honestly, a huge amount of human creativity works this way too. Jazz came from combining blues, ragtime, African rhythms, and European harmony. The iPhone combined a phone, a music player, and a touchscreen. Most breakthroughs are novel recombinations of things that already existed. AI is genuinely good at this, and it can help you do it faster and from more angles than you’d reach on your own.

But then there’s the other kind — imagining something out of nothing. Something that doesn’t reference what came before. AI actually does do emergence. Like, when models get big enough, capabilities show up that nobody explicitly programmed. A model suddenly can do chain-of-thought reasoning or handle tasks it was never specifically trained on. 

We don’t fully understand why it happens. We can observe it — new capabilities appearing out of scale and complexity — but we can’t confidently say it’s just existing patterns recombining in clever ways. We don’t understand model weights well enough to make that claim.

But with humans, emergence comes from a place that’s not just computational. The first person who looked at the sky and wrote a poem wasn’t remixing existing art forms — they were inventing a category of human expression that didn’t exist before. That impulse came from somewhere embodied, intuitive, maybe even dimensional in ways we haven’t mapped. You can’t trace it back to inputs. It just arose.

Humans can wake up tomorrow with an idea that has no precedent, no ancestry in prior knowledge. It just appears. From wherever that source is that nobody can point to. With AI emergence, we genuinely don’t know what’s going on under the hood — it could be more than simple pattern recombination, we can’t say for sure. But with human emergence, it seems to come from somewhere that isn’t just data at all. And that feels like a different kind of new. Even if we can’t fully prove why yet.

## the double mystery

We can’t fully explain how human consciousness works yet. Neuroscience can map brain regions. It can track neural pathways. But the hard question — why does subjective experience exist? why does it feel like something to be you? — that’s unsolved. We know the brain does it. We don’t know how. We can’t point to a neuron and say “this is where your sense of self lives.”

And on the AI side, there’s an eerily parallel problem. We built these systems that work remarkably well, but we can’t fully explain why. The interpretability question — what do the model’s weights actually represent? — is still wide open. We know the model understands things. We can’t point to where or how.

So think about that for a second. We’re trying to figure out if we can make AI conscious. But we can’t explain consciousness in ourselves. And we can’t fully interpret the systems we’ve already built. We’re building on top of two unknowns.

And from what I’ve been learning about consciousness, it might not even be a purely physical phenomenon. It might be more dimensional than we currently understand — which means our current scientific frameworks might not be enough to capture it. Maybe quantum physics holds some answers. Maybe it’s something beyond even that. If consciousness operates on levels we haven’t figured out how to measure yet, then replicating it in a machine is a way bigger question than the AI discourse usually makes it sound.

## the hopeful part though

Here’s where I land with all of this. And it’s actually kind of hopeful.

The more advanced AI gets, the more it highlights what only humans bring. Not less. That innate pull toward what matters to you — AI doesn’t have it. The ability to look at your own patterns and consciously choose to change — AI can’t do it. That gut feeling, that embodied knowing, that emotional intelligence that fires before your logical mind even catches up — AI has no access to any of that.

What AI does have is an insane depth of accumulated knowledge — everything humans have ever written and said, compressed into something you can have a conversation with. And that’s genuinely valuable. It’s like a pseudo-mentor with centuries of knowledge.

The magic is in the combination. You bring the direction, the intuition, the agency, the creative spark. AI brings the breadth. And what comes out of that collaboration is going to be different for every single person, because every person brings a different inner world to the conversation.

The most important human capacity — that mysterious thing that makes you _you_ , the thing that precedes data and transcends it — that can’t be captured in a snapshot.
