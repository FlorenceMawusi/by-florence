# My Personal Resume
_This one won’t get me hired. _

We all have two resumes.

There’s the one you send to recruiters. The polished one. The one where every bullet point starts with a power verb and ends with a metric. “Increased revenue by 200%.” “Led a cross-functional team of 100,000 lol.” “Deployed a system serving 2M users.”

And then there’s the real one. The one that lives in your body. The one that nobody asks for in an interview but is honestly the reason you’re any good at what you do.

**This is that resume:**

### _My Personal Resume_

### Summary

Spent the better part of a decade solving problems nobody gave me a manual for. Specializes in sitting with discomfort until it turns into clarity. Known for breaking things, learning from the wreckage, and coming back with something better. Currently in the process of unlearning everything that made me “productive” and relearning what makes me _alive_.

* * *

**Experience**

**Chief Boundary Setter** | My Own Life | Ongoing

Identified a lifelong pattern of people-pleasing and launched a multi-year initiative to dismantle it. Consumed approximately 47 self-help books before finding the one that actually worked. Successfully told a loved one “no” for the first time without writing a three-page justification. Ongoing project. No end date. That’s the point.

**Senior Failure Analyst** | Every Job I’ve Ever Had

Shipped things that broke. Said “I don’t know” in rooms where everyone else was pretending they did. Learned that the person who asks the “dumb” question is usually the one everybody else was too scared to be. Discovered that my best work shows up when nobody’s watching and my worst work shows up when everybody is.

**Immigrant & Life Relocator** | Then → Now

Moved my entire life somewhere new with no guarantees. Navigated systems that were foreign. Built a career, a reputation, and a sense of self in a place that requires me to prove my worth on paper every single year. Still here. Still going.

**Sovereignty Researcher** | Independent Study | Ongoing

Self-directed curriculum spanning assertiveness, consciousness, game theory, power structures, and what it actually means to be free. No degree in any of it. No institution. Just curiosity and a stubborn refusal to stop asking why. Published findings on a blog. Peer review will consist of people in my DMs saying “I needed this today.”

**Relationship & Friendship Engineer** | Various Humans | Ongoing

Entered relationships and friendships with zero blueprints. Developed real-time conflict resolution skills, the ability to say “that hurt me” without the world ending, and a framework for figuring out when to compromise and when to hold your ground. Learned that love — any kind of love — is not the absence of friction but the willingness to stay in the room and figure it out.

**Accidental Entrepreneur** | Honestly Didn’t Plan This

Built a tool in one sitting that would have taken someone else hours. Got called a “developer” by people who had no idea what that meant but were impressed anyway. Started and ended a business. Still figuring out taxes. Probably always will be.

* * *

**Skills**

Sitting with not knowing. Trying things that might not work. Asking for help after years of thinking I shouldn’t need it. Writing about hard things in simple words. Making breakthroughs on attempt number twenty. Rebuilding myself after the attempts that didn’t work.

* * *

**Education**

Life. Mostly.

Also some degrees. But honestly the life part taught me more.

* * *

**References**

Available upon request. But probably just my journal entries, some late-night conversations with people I trust, and the version of me from five years ago who wouldn’t even recognize who I am now.

* * *

_Here’s the thing._

Every impressive line on my professional resume has a messy, human, unglamorous story behind it. The system I “deployed”? I almost gave up on it twice. The team I “led”? Half the time I was figuring it out alongside them and hoping nobody noticed. The “results” I “drove”? They came after a bunch of results I didn’t drive. Results that flopped. Results that went nowhere.

But we don’t put that on the resume, do we?

We put the highlight reel. The clean version. The version where it looks like we knew what we were doing the whole time.

And I get it. Recruiters have 30 seconds. They need the metrics. They need the keywords. That’s the game.

But I think something gets lost when the only version of ourselves we ever put on paper is the one where we never struggled. Because struggling is where the actual skill came from. The resilience, the creativity, the ability to figure things out when nothing makes sense — that didn’t come from the wins. It came from all the things that didn’t work first.

So yeah. This resume won’t get me hired.

But it’s the one I’m most proud of.
